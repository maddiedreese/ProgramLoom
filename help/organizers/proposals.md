# Collect proposals

A call for proposals gives people a clear, public way to suggest sessions for your event.

## Build the form

Open your event, then open **Call for proposals**. Add only the questions your team will use. Common questions include:

- session title and description;
- track or topic;
- format, such as talk or workshop;
- intended audience;
- speaker biography and organization; and
- any equipment or accessibility needs.

Use a conditional question when it applies only to one answer. For example, a workshop can ask about participant prerequisites without showing that question to every talk submitter.

## Publish the form

Choose **Publish CFP** when the form, opening date, and deadline are ready. ProgramLoom gives you a public link. Anyone with the link can read the event details and submit while the form is open.

## Work with submissions

Open **Submissions** to:

- search by title, speaker, organization, or description;
- show the columns that matter to your team;
- combine filters for status, track, format, reviewer, score, decision, date, tags, and custom answers;
- save a personal view or share a view with the organization; and
- select records for a careful bulk action.

When a view has changed since it was saved, ProgramLoom tells you. Save the update if the new layout should become the reusable view.

## Route proposals automatically

Routing rules can send a proposal to the right reviewer group based on its form, track, format, tags, or answers. ProgramLoom previews the affected proposals and eligible reviewers before you run a rule.

Reviewers who are at capacity, have a conflict, have recused themselves, or would review their own proposal are skipped. Running the same routing rules again does not create duplicate assignments.

## Close submissions

When the deadline arrives, choose **Close submissions**. Submitted proposals remain available to organizers and reviewers. Submitters can no longer create or change proposals unless an organizer reopens the form or grants an allowed edit path.
